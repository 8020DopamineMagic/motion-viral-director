## 1. Two-speed strategy and the format matrix (Heuristic strategy, Platform specs)

Short-form wins discovery and reach. Long-form builds trust, search durability and watch time. Run both, and do not collapse their pacing into one template.

| | Short (0:15-3:00) | Medium (1:00-5:00) | Long (5:00-20:00+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary job | Feed discovery, scroll-stop, completion, rewatch loops, DM shares | Tight product explainers, brand stories, self-contained mini-docs | Deep education, narrative immersion, authority, TV and desktop session time |
| Aspect ratios | 9:16 (1080 x 1920). Shorts up to 3 min, Reels, TikTok | 9:16, 4:5 (1080 x 1350), 1:1 (1080 x 1080) or 16:9 | 16:9 (1920 x 1080 or 3840 x 2160), 2.39:1 for cinema |
| Opening window | 0-3 s stacked hook, readable on mute at 0.0 s | 0-10 s thesis plus proof | 0-30 s thumbnail and title match, plus one macro curiosity loop |
| Pacing | Pattern interrupts roughly every 2-4 s; zero preamble | Tight escalation with 1-2 breathing holds | Tension-and-release waves, quiet pauses between acts |
| Archetypes | Speed-run tutorials, POV and pain mirrors, motion hooks, narrative teasers | Product launches, case studies, concise visual short films | Video essays, documentaries, tutorials, deep-dive commentary |

The 2-4 s interval is a heuristic, not a rule. A held shot that is doing emotional work is not a pacing failure.

## 2. Platform ranking signals (Platform; re-verify before relying on them)

Dates and status for each item are in `freshness-ledger.md`.

**YouTube Shorts.** The feed's first gate is *viewed versus swiped away*, alongside average percentage viewed, rewatches and viewer satisfaction surveys. Since 31 March 2025 YouTube counts **Views** as every start or loop with no watch-time threshold, and reports **Engaged views** separately; engaged views are the figure used for Partner Program eligibility and for comparing like with like. Shorts are searchable, so keyword-rich titles and descriptions matter. Watermarked reposts and badly cropped horizontal clips are suppressed.

**Instagram Reels.** Adam Mosseri (head of Instagram) named the top three ranking signals in January 2025: **watch time**, **likes per reach** and **sends per reach**. Likes weigh slightly more for connected reach (followers); sends weigh more for unconnected reach (non-followers), which makes DM shares the growth signal. Judge **rates**, not raw counts: sends divided by reach. Comments are not in the top three.

Any exact multiplier ("a send is worth five likes") is creator speculation. Do not repeat it as fact.

**TikTok.** Practitioners track micro-retention at 1 s (is the subject or result visible?), 3 s (is the core question clear?), the midpoint, and completion, rewatch and share behaviour at the end (Heuristic). TikTok indexes caption text and spoken words for search, so the first spoken sentence should carry the keyword.

## 3. Short-form stacked hook, 0.0-3.0 s (Heuristic, widely used)

1. **0.0-0.5 s, visual trigger.** Hard cut on frame 0. No fade-in, no logo bumper. Kinetic movement, a before/after contrast, or a bold 5-8 word headline readable on mute.
2. **0.5-1.5 s, verbal trigger.** One sharp sentence with the primary topic keyword plus tension, curiosity or proof.
3. **1.5-3.0 s, context lock.** Who this is for and the exact payoff coming. Backstory only after second 5.

**Seven hook frameworks.** Pick the one the content can honestly pay off; a hook that over-promises produces a spike of swipes at the reveal.

| Framework | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Shock to logic | A startling claim, then the reason it is true | You have a counter-intuitive fact and can prove it fast |
| Specific result | A concrete number or outcome first | You have a measurable before and after |
| Open loop | A question or unfinished event that needs resolving | The payoff is a reveal |
| Hot take | A position some of the audience will disagree with | You can defend it, and argument helps reach |
| POV / relatable pain | The viewer's own frustrating moment, shown | The audience shares one clear pain |
| Speed-run tutorial | "X in N seconds" plus the first step already happening | The value is a method |
| Proof first | The finished result on screen at 0.0 s | The output is visually impressive |

Write two or three hooks from different frameworks and offer them as options; the first one drafted is rarely the best.

## 4. Long-form opening, 0-30 s (Heuristic)

- Confirm the thumbnail and title visually in the first frames. Viewers arrive sceptical, and a mismatch reads as bait.
- Use visual variety early. No greeting, no channel intro.
- Establish stakes: why this matters and to whom.
- Open one macro curiosity gap that the rest of the video closes.

## 5. Anti-drop-off mechanics (Heuristic)

- **Remove segmentation loss.** Signpost phrases like "moving on to step two" give the viewer a clean psychological exit. Bridge instead: introduce the picture or the conflict of beat B while the last sentence of beat A finishes. A J-cut does this in sound.
- **Schedule tension-and-release resets.** In anything over 2 minutes, plan deliberate quiet pauses and music shifts between acts. Unbroken stimulation tires people and they leave.
- **Nest open loops.** One macro question opened in seconds 0-5; micro-loops closed every 15-45 s to reward attention; the final answer withheld until the emotional climax.

## 6. Loop engineering for Shorts (Heuristic)

Write the last clause so it joins grammatically and rhythmically to frame 0. Done well, viewers watch 2-3 seconds of the replay before noticing the seam, which lifts average percentage viewed past 100%.

Example: the piece opens on "Your spreadsheet missed $14,200 last quarter." and ends "...which is why, every quarter," so the cut back to frame 0 completes the sentence. Match the audio too: the last frame's sound bed should cut cleanly into the first frame's.

Only loop when the content supports it. A forced loop that withholds the ending feels like a trick.

## 7. Walter Murch's Rule of Six (Craft)

When a cut forces a compromise, protect the higher-ranked criteria first.

| Rank | Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emotion: is it true to the feeling of the moment? | 51% |
| 2 | Story: does it advance the story? | 23% |
| 3 | Rhythm: does it land at the right moment? | 10% |
| 4 | Eye-trace: does it respect where the viewer is looking? | 7% |
| 5 | Two-dimensional plane of screen | 5% |
| 6 | Three-dimensional space of action | 4% |

Never give up an emotionally true cut to fix a small continuity mismatch. In an AV script, annotate key cuts with the criterion they serve.

## 8. Retention-curve diagnostics

Read the shape first, then decide. Platform analytics supply the curve; the causes and fixes below are practitioner heuristics, so confirm against comments and the frames themselves.

| Curve pattern | Most likely cause | Check first | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low intro retention, high swipe-away | Packaging or first-frame promise mismatch | Does frame 0 show what the thumbnail and title promised? Is there a fade or bumper? | Change the hook or the thumbnail and title **before** abandoning the topic |
| Gradual, steady decline | Predictable pacing; one idea stretched too long | Where does the visual grammar stop changing? | Add a midpoint reversal, challenge or visual shift; cut the repeated point |
| Sharp dip at one moment | Dead air, filler, or a hard topic exit ("moving on to...") | The exact sentence and frame at the dip | Trim hard, or put a pattern interrupt there (zoom, B-roll, sound shift); bridge the seam |
| Spike (above-baseline rewatch) | Either high rewatch value, or confusion forcing a replay | Comments and frame clarity at that timestamp | If it is value, do more of it; if it is confusion, slow down or add a caption |
| Best moment arrives late | The strongest material is buried | Timestamp of the top moment against audience remaining | Pull the reveal forward as a cold-open teaser before the audience has thinned |
| Cliff in the final seconds | Outro signals ("thanks for watching") or a visible wind-down | Last 10% of the script | End on the payoff; cut the goodbye; loop if short-form |

Order of operations when a video underperforms: packaging and first frame, then the first 30 s (or 3 s), then the worst single dip, then overall pacing. Fixing pacing on a video nobody clicks is wasted work.

## 9. What to ask for before diagnosing

A diagnosis without data is a guess, so say which of these you have and which you are assuming:

- the retention graph or its key numbers (percentage at 0:03, 0:30, midpoint, end; any spikes or dips with timestamps);
- viewed versus swiped away (Shorts), or click-through rate and average view duration (long-form);
- the script or transcript with timecodes;
- the thumbnail and title;
- for Reels, sends per reach and likes per reach.

If only some are available, diagnose from what is there, name the missing evidence under pending checks, and give the cheapest test that would confirm the diagnosis.
